Two unrelated programs, same pipeline

APPLE · 1987–2004 · MACINTOSH

HyperCard

  • 10 user-made stacks from the Internet Archive, not the app
  • Both file formats: 2.x (9 stacks) and 1.x (1 stack)
  • 1-bit pictures in a compressed, undocumented codec
  • Scripts in HyperTalk (288 scripts), rendered in pixels

EPIC MEGAGAMES · 1991 · MS-DOS

ZZT

  • 8 community-made world files, Museum of ZZT, not the app
  • Run-length-encoded grids of text characters
  • Scripts in ZZT-OOP (5,227 programs)
  • Rendered in character cells, playable game

My professor chose HyperCard; I chose ZZT to test generality. Only files people made with each program were analyzed, never the programs themselves. Epic’s own ZZT files are excluded: their license forbids reverse engineering.

To show the system is general, I ran it on two programs that have nothing in common. HyperCard is Apple’s original 1987 Mac tool, sold until 2004, with pixel graphics and a language called HyperTalk. The agents never looked at the HyperCard application: they worked on stacks, the documents people made with it, like analyzing Word files without opening Word. The corpus covers both of its file formats: nine stacks from HyperCard 2.x and one older stack, Inigo Gets Out, in the 1.x format, and the reader handles both. ZZT is a 1991 DOS game maker: text-mode graphics, its own scripting language, and thousands of worlds made by the community. One ethics note: ZZT’s license forbids reverse engineering Epic’s own files, so I only used worlds made by other people, and the agents never touched the ZZT program itself.
